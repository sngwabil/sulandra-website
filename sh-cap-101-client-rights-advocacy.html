Section 2: Action Demonstrations in Daily Care

Client rights must be visible in practical daily actions.

✖ Restrictive approach

Entering without knocking, choosing clothing without consent, restricting food for staff convenience, or discussing private information in shared spaces.

✓ Advocacy approach

Knocking and waiting, presenting meaningful choices, supporting safe access, explaining options, honoring preferences, and protecting confidentiality.

Section 4: Case Study — Empowering Autonomy

Background: Marcus expresses frustration because staff select the television programming and determine snack times without resident input. Staff say the practice keeps the home orderly.

Rights-based analysis: Convenience does not replace person-centered choice. Staff should facilitate house meetings, collaborative schedules, accessible choices, individualized alternatives, and documentation of preferences. Safety limits must be individualized, justified, approved, and never used merely for staff convenience.

Key practice: Support informed choice, document the person’s preference, explain genuine safety considerations, and involve the individual in resolving shared-living conflicts.
